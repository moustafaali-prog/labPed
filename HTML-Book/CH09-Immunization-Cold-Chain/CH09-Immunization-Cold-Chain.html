1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, the student will be able to:

  1. Distinguish live-attenuated vaccines from inactivated/recombinant vaccines and apply the route and needle/site rules for each.
  2. Describe the recommended (EPI-type) childhood immunization schedule and apply the current national schedule.
  3. List the vaccine contraindications and precautions (severe febrile illness, severe immunocompromise, anaphylaxis to vaccine/component) and correct common misconceptions.
  4. Manage the cold chain: storage at +2 to +8 °C (center shelf, protected from light/freezing), expiry and vaccine-vial-monitor checks, and "do not use" rule when temperature control is doubtful.
  5. Administer IM/SC vaccines (vastus lateralis in infants), provide atraumatic care, document, and manage vaccine reactions including anaphylaxis.

2. Placement Box

4. Principles of Immunization

  • Vaccines for routine immunizations are among the safest and most reliable drugs available; minor side effects occur, and serious reactions are rare.
  • Vaccines may contain inactive components (preservatives, stabilizers, adjuvants, antibiotics such as neomycin, and culture-medium proteins such as egg) — the child may react to these rather than to the vaccine itself.
  • Types:
  • Live-attenuated (e.g., MMR, varicella, rotavirus, BCG): multiplication of the virus may be enhanced in the immunocompromised → generally not given to severely immunocompromised persons.
  • Inactivated/recombinant (e.g., DTaP, HepB, IPV, Hib, PCV, influenza): cannot replicate; side effects typically local and occur within hours–days.
  • HepB is a recombinant vaccine made by splicing a gene into yeast cultures; a severe yeast hypersensitivity would preclude its use without specialist review.

6. The Cold Chain and Vaccine Storage

  • Proper vaccine storage is critical to vaccine efficacy. If you suspect a vaccine was not maintained at the proper storage temperature, do not use it — an ineffective vaccine does not prevent disease.
  • Standard cold-chain temperature: +2 to +8 °C (as per national cold-chain policy). Protect vaccines from freezing and from light.
  • Placement: store vaccine bottles on a center shelf of the refrigerator, not in the door, where frequent temperature changes on opening can alter potency.
  • Protect light-sensitive vials by wrapping in aluminum foil.
  • Keep the refrigerator temperature log (twice-daily recording) and ensure no vaccine is used after its expiration date.
  • Follow the manufacturer's directions for storage and reconstitution for each vaccine (diluent, mixing, and time limits after reconstitution).

7. Contraindications and Precautions

Contraindications (do not give):

  • Severe febrile illness — the general contraindication for all immunizations.
  • Severe/unknown immunocompromise — for live vaccines (MMR, varicella, rotavirus, BCG).
  • Pregnancy — for MMR; breastfeeding is not a contraindication for any vaccine.
  • Recent passive immunity: MMR and varicella postponed ≥3 months after immunoglobulin/blood transfusions (except washed red cells).
  • Anaphylaxis to a previous vaccine or a component (e.g., anaphylaxis to neomycin, gelatin) — proper contraindication.

NOT contraindications (common misconceptions):

  • Minor illnesses such as the common cold are not a contraindication.
  • Egg sensitivity is not a contraindication for MMR or inactivated influenza.
  • Family history of seizures/SIDS, penicillin allergy, and neomycin contact dermatitis are not contraindications.
  • A precaution is a condition that might increase the risk; give the vaccine only if benefit outweighs risk (e.g., a moderate acute illness with fever).

8. Administration Technique

Routes by vaccine type : IM — DTaP/DT/Tdap, HepA/HepB, Hib, PCV, HPV, MCV4, influenza (RIV/IIV); SC — MMR, IPV, varicella, MPSV4; intranasal — influenza LAIV.

  • The dorsogluteal site is not recommended for children (sciatic nerve risk).
  • Aspiration for blood is no longer recommended for IM injections — large vessels are absent at recommended sites, and aspiration causes more pain.
  • Liquid adjuvant vaccines (e.g., DTaP) need excellent IM technique; subcutaneous/intracutaneous injection causes local irritation or abscess.
  • Rotate sites with repeated doses.

Atraumatic care :

  • EMLA (1 h) or LMX4 (30 min) topical anesthetic under an occlusive dressing before injection.
  • Beneficial positioning — parent holds the infant; older children sit upright.
  • Breastfeeding during/just before injections reduces pain.
  • Distraction ("blow, blow, blow"), calm nurses, neutral words ("here I go"), and no site massage.

Emergency preparedness : Before any vaccine, have the anaphylaxis kit ready:

  • EpiPen Jr 0.15 mg (0.3 mL of 1:2000) IM for a child 15–30 kg
  • EpiPen 0.3 mg (0.3 mL of 1:1000) IM for a child ≥30 kg.

9. Nursing Care Plan (Vaccination Session)

Assessment: 2-month-old infant due for Pentavalent-1, OPV-1, PCV-1, Rotavirus-1. No fever, no known allergies, thriving.

NANDA diagnosis: Risk for injury (vaccine reaction) / Deficient knowledge (parents) related to immunization schedule and side effects.

Goals: Safe administration; parents informed; normal side effects managed; serious reactions detected immediately.

Interventions (with rationale):

  1. Verify schedule, vaccine, expiry, and cold-chain record — prevents using ineffective vaccine.
  2. Take an allergy/history screen for contraindications — prevents adverse reaction.
  3. Provide the vaccine information statement (VIS) and answer questions before giving — informed consent.
  4. Prepare the emergency tray (EpiPen Jr) before injection — immediate anaphylaxis management.
  5. Give IM vaccines at vastus lateralis, 25 g ⅝–1 in, no aspiration; offer breastfeeding during — reduces pain and reactions.
  6. Observe ≥20–30 min after the session; document site, lot, route, and dose — documentation and prompt response.
  7. Evaluate: no serious reaction; parent can state the next due date and expected mild side effects.

11. Procedure — Hospital Step

  • Record the HepB birth dose before discharge for every newborn (mother HBsAg negative).
  • During the session, keep the emergency tray (EpiPen Jr, EpiPen) within reach.
  • Document each vaccine in the child's immunization card and the ward record: date, vaccine, dose, route, site, lot number, and expiry; update the next-visit date.
  • Report suspected cold-chain breaks (temperature outside +2 to +8 °C, freeze, or VVM change) to the supervising nurse; quarantine, do not use.

12. Case Study (Cold Chain Breach)

Scenario: A student nurse opens the vaccination refrigerator and finds the temperature log shows 9.5 °C for the morning reading, and a vial of MMR reconstituted 3 hours earlier is still in the tray.

  1. What should be done with the MMR vial? (Discard/quarantine — beyond its reconstitution time limit per the package insert; do not use.)
  2. How should the suspected cold-chain breach be handled? (Report to the supervising nurse/pharmacist; quarantine stock; document; do not use vaccines suspected of exposure.)
  3. Which placement rule was violated by storing in the door? (Vaccines must be on the center shelf; the door exposes them to temperature changes on opening.)
  4. Would MMR cause harm to a severely immunocompromised child? (Yes — a live vaccine; enhanced viral multiplication can cause severe vaccine-induced illness.)

14. Key Points

  • Vaccines are safe but need correct route, site, needle, and cold chain to be effective.
  • Cold chain +2 to +8 °C, center shelf, protect from light/freezing, check expiry, follow manufacturer's reconstitution directions.
  • Do not use any vaccine suspected of improper temperature exposure.
  • Contraindications: severe febrile illness, severe/unknown immunocompromise (live vaccines), pregnancy (MMR), anaphylaxis to vaccine/component; minor illness, egg allergy, and breastfeeding are NOT contraindications.
  • IM: vastus lateralis in infants; no dorsogluteal; no aspiration; atraumatic care (EMLA, positioning, breastfeeding, distraction).
  • Emergency: EpiPen Jr 0.15 mg (15–30 kg) / EpiPen 0.3 mg (≥30 kg) IM.
