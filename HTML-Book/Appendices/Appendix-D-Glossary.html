Appendix D — Glossary of Key Terms (English–English)

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Book: Clinical Pediatric Nursing: A Comprehensive Practical Guide

Appendix D — alphabetized glossary of the technical terms used in the ward chapters and procedures.

TermDefinition
AganglionosisAbsence of the enteric ganglion cells (Auerbach/Meissner plexuses) — the cause of Hirschsprung disease [CH18].
AtelectasisCollapse of small areas of lung; in RDS caused by surfactant deficiency [CH12].
Attraumatic careCare that minimizes physical and psychological stress (pain, separation, loss of control) [CH1, CH3].
ChelationRemoval of excess iron from the body (deferoxamine/deferasirox) in thalassemia [CH13].
CNS prophylaxisPreventive treatment of the central nervous system (intrathecal chemotherapy) in ALL [CH14].
Cold chainThe refrigerated system (2–8 °C) that preserves vaccine potency from manufacturer to use [CH9].
ColostomyAn opening of the colon onto the abdominal wall for stool drainage [CH18, 20.14].
Consolidation(a) Chemotherapy phase in ALL; (b) solidification of lung tissue in pneumonia [CH14, CH16].
DDAVP (desmopressin)Drug that raises factor VIII/vWF; used in mild hemophilia A and von Willebrand disease [CH15].
DeferasiroxOral iron chelator for thalassemia; alternatives: deferoxamine (SC), deferiprone (PO) [CH13].
EnterocolitisSevere inflammatory complication of Hirschsprung disease (fever, distension, loose bloody stools) [CH18].
Exchange transfusionReplacement of the infant's blood to lower bilirubin/antibody load [CH11].
Factor concentratePurified clotting factor VIII or IX given IV for bleeding in hemophilia [CH15].
Gavage feedingFeeding by a tube (naso/orogastric) when the infant cannot feed safely by mouth [20.7].
Ground-glass appearanceHazy lung opacity on the RDS chest radiograph [CH12].
GruntingForced expiration against a partially closed glottis — a respiratory distress sign [CH12, CH16].
HemarthrosisBleeding into a joint — the most common bleeding manifestation of hemophilia [CH15].
HyperbilirubinemiaElevation of serum bilirubin; physiologic vs pathologic [CH11].
HypochloremiaLow serum chloride — characteristic of HPS alkalosis [CH19].
InductionThe first intensive chemotherapy phase of ALL [CH14].
Infantile reflexesAutomatic responses of the newborn (Moro, rooting, grasp) that disappear with maturation [CH6].
IntrathecalInto the cerebrospinal fluid (e.g., methotrexate for CNS prophylaxis) [CH14].
KernicterusBilirubin encephalopathy — permanent brain damage from very high unconjugated bilirubin [CH11].
MaintenanceProlonged lower-intensity phase of ALL chemotherapy [CH14].
Metabolic alkalosisElevated blood pH/bicarbonate from loss of gastric acid — classic in HPS [CH19].
NeutropeniaLow neutrophil count → high infection risk (ALL chemotherapy) [CH14].
Olive-shaped massThe palpable hypertrophied pylorus in HPS [CH19].
ORSOral rehydration solution — 75 mmol/L sodium + 13.5 g/L glucose [CH17, 20.10].
PhototherapyBlue-green light therapy that converts bilirubin to exercisable isomers [CH11, 20.9].
Projectile vomitingForceful, nonbilious vomiting that travels a distance — classic of HPS [CH19].
Pull-throughDefinitive surgical repair of Hirschsprung disease (Swenson, Duhamel, Soave) [CH18].
PyloromyotomyFredet–Ramstedt operation: longitudinal division of the pyloric muscle in HPS [CH19].
RICERest, Ice, Compression, Elevation — bleeding-joint first aid in hemophilia [CH15].
RetractionsInward pulling of the chest wall with breathing (subcostal, intercostal, suprasternal) [CH12, CH16].
SurfactantSurface-active agent that lowers alveolar surface tension; deficient in RDS [CH12].
TachypneaAbnormally fast respiratory rate [CH5, CH12].
Target cellsAbnormally flat RBCs with central hemoglobin dot — seen in thalassemia [CH13].
Transition zoneThe segment between normal and aganglionic bowel in Hirschsprung disease [CH18].
Turgor (skin)Skin elasticity used in dehydration assessment (tenting = reduced turgor) [CH17, App. B].
Vastus lateralisThe preferred pediatric IM injection site (middle third of the anterolateral thigh) [CH8, 20.13].
