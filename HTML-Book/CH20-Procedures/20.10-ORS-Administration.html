Procedure 20.10 — ORS Administration and Reassessment

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Rehydration skill · Time: ≈15 min (4-hour plan) · Applies to: child with mild/moderate dehydration · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Rehydrate the child with dehydration using oral rehydration solution (ORT) and reassess to prevent progression to hypovolemic shock (see Chapter 17).

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • Weight and the current dehydration grade (mild / moderate / severe).
  • Hydration indicators: skin turgor, oral mucosa, tears, fontanel, pulse, urine output.
  • Ability to drink and presence of vomiting (vomiting is not a contraindication).

Equipment — What you need

  • ORS packet/mix + clean water, cup, spoon or oral syringe, basin, graduated measuring cup, weight scale, chart.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Weigh the child; assess and grade dehydration (mild/moderate/severe).The grade determines the ORS rate and the need for IV therapy.☐
2Prepare fresh ORS per the packet instructions (correct water amount; discard after the stated period).Correct dilution and freshness are essential for safe rehydration.☐
3Calculate the 4-hour volume (50 or 100 mL/kg) and per-stool replacement (10 mL/kg).Volumes are weight-based and re-checked against the grade.☐
4Offer ORS 5–10 mL every 2–5 min by spoon/syringe; continue despite vomiting.Small frequent amounts are tolerated even when vomiting.☐
5After the first hour, offer larger amounts as tolerated; keep a strict intake record.Increasing amounts replace losses once tolerance improves.☐
6Reassess hydration every 4–8 h (turgor, mucosa, tears, fontanel, pulse, urine output) and weigh daily.Reassessment detects failure of oral rehydration early.☐
7If ORT fails (vomits all, worsening lethargy, no urine): stop ORT, notify the physician, prepare for IV fluids.Failure of oral therapy requires the IV route.☐
8Continue breastfeeding/formula; reintroduce the normal diet early; avoid juice/high-sugar drinks.Early feeding shortens illness; high-sugar drinks worsen diarrhea.☐
9Teach the caregiver ORS preparation, dosing, and the danger signs to return.Caregiver competence sustains rehydration at home.☐
Administering ORS with a syringe to a dehydrated infant

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • ORS composition: 75 mmol/L sodium and 13.5 g/L glucose.
  • Volume plan: mild 50 mL/kg, moderate 100 mL/kg within 4 h.
  • Replace each diarrheal stool with ORS 10 mL/kg; expected urine output 0.5–1 mL/kg/h.

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Tap water, milk, juice, soup, or broth are not appropriate for rehydration.
  • Severe dehydration: IV lactated Ringer 40 mL/kg (bolus 20 mL/kg then reassess) until pulse/consciousness normalize, then ORS.
  • Danger signs requiring return: vomiting everything, worsening lethargy, no urine, blood in stool.

Documentation — What to record

  • Fluid intake by mouth/IV, stool/emesis output, urine output, daily weight, and reassessment findings.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student prepares ORS correctly, performs the 4-hour reassessment, and recognizes ORT failure requiring escalation.
