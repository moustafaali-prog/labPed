Part VI — Core Clinical Procedures, Chapter 20

1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, the student will be able to:

  1. Perform the 15 core ward procedures listed below safely, in the correct order, and with the correct equipment for the child's age.
  2. Apply the principles of standard and transmission-based precautions to every procedure.
  3. Calculate pediatric drug doses and IV infusion/ORS volumes correctly and recheck the arithmetic.
  4. Verify tube, cannula, and blood-product placement/identity before feeding, infusing, or transfusing.
  5. Document every procedure (route, site, volumes, responses) and recognize the danger signs that require immediate reporting.

2. Placement Box

Part VI contains the practical "how-to" core every ward nurse performs daily. It consolidates the technical skills taught across Parts I–V (hand hygiene, vitals, injections, ORS, phototherapy, transfusions, surgery) into a single competency checklist. Each procedure has its own sub-file with a full purpose → principles → equipment → step checklist → figure → documentation → verification structure.

5. General Principles That Apply to All Procedures

  • Attraumatic care (CH1): prepare the child/age-appropriate, use distraction and local/anesthetic comfort before painful steps, and involve the parents.
  • Infection control first (20.1): hand hygiene and correct PPE before every task.
  • Two-nurse check for dose-critical steps — drug doses, IV rates, ORS volumes, and blood products (20.12, 20.13).
  • Verify before acting: NG placement before feeding (20.7), oxygen flow at the patient end (20.8), transfusion identity by two staff (20.12).
  • Document continuously: route, site, gauges, volumes, temperature, and the child's response; use the I&O and vital-sign flow sheets.

7. Multiple-Choice Questions (Key at the end)

  1. The single most important practice to prevent cross-infection in the ward is:

A. wearing a gown for every procedure · B. hand hygiene · C. isolation of every admitted child · D. fumigation of the ward.

  1. The first bath of the newborn should be delayed until:

A. the cord falls off · B. the baby is 24 h old · C. temperature is stable for at least 2–4 h · D. meconium is passed.

  1. For a gavage feed, the feeding tube is measured from:

A. tip of the nose to the umbilicus · B. nose → ear → xiphoid (NEX) · C. mouth to the sternal notch · D. ear lobe to the ankle.

  1. Gavage feeding is temporarily contraindicated in the infant who:

A. is jaundiced · B. is <1500 g · C. has marked tachypnea/apnea (aspiration risk) · D. is receiving phototherapy.

  1. The correct cuff width for BP in a child is about:

A. 40% of the upper-arm circumference · B. half the arm length · C. the full diameter of the antecubital fossa · D. one-third of the thigh.

  1. In moderate dehydration, the ORS volume (replacement + maintenance) given over 4 h is approximately:

A. 10 mL/kg · B. 25 mL/kg · C. 100 mL/kg · D. 300 mL/kg.

  1. A newborn's axillary temperature of 38.5 °C should be:

A. rechecked in 6 h · B. treated with antipyretics by the nurse · C. reported immediately as a medical concern · D. ignored because newborns run warm.

  1. Which IV set is preferred in pediatrics for precise rate control?

A. macrodrip 15 gtt/mL · B. glass burette · C. microdrip 60 gtt/mL or infusion pump · D. drip chamber without calibration.

  1. If a transfusion reaction is suspected, the FIRST action is:

A. continue at a slower rate · B. give antihistamine · C. stop the transfusion, keep the line open with normal saline, and notify the physician · D. warm the blood.

  1. The preferred IM site in infants <3 years is:

A. dorsogluteal · B. deltoid · C. vastus lateralis · D. rectus femoris.

Key: 1-B, 2-C, 3-B, 4-C, 5-A, 6-C, 7-C, 8-C, 9-C, 10-C.

8. Key Points

  • Fifteen procedures make up the Chapter 20 competency checklist; each is trained in the skills lab then observed on the ward.
  • Calculation errors are the most common medication-error cause in children — always recalculate and double-check doses, rates, and volumes.
  • Verification (placement, identity, patency) and documentation are non-negotiable parts of every procedure.
