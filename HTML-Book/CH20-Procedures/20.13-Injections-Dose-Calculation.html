Procedure 20.13 — SC, IM, and ID Injections plus Drug-Dose Calculation

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Medication administration · Time: ≈10 min · Applies to: all ages (site by age) · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Administer medications safely by subcutaneous (SC), intramuscular (IM), and intradermal (ID) routes in children.
  • Verify that every dose is calculated and checked before administration.

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • The 8 rights: right patient, drug, dose, route, time, documentation, assessment, right to refuse/education.
  • Weight-based dose (mg/kg) vs the ordered dose — recheck the arithmetic with a second staff member.
  • Site by age: vastus lateralis preferred <3 years; deltoid after ≈15 months with enough muscle; dorsogluteal avoided in children.

Equipment — What you need

  • Correct syringe (1 mL tuberculin for ID/subunit vaccines; 2–3 mL IM), correct-gauge needle (IM 22–25 G; SC 25–26 G; ID 26–27 G), alcohol swab, gloves, sharps container, drug/vial and the original order.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Calculate the dose: ordered mg ÷ available mg/mL = volume (mL); recheck against the mg/kg recommendation and have it double-checked.Volume (mL) = ordered dose (mg) ÷ drug concentration (mg/mL) — arithmetic errors are the main cause of drug errors.☐
2Verify the 8 rights using the original order + physician/doc read-back; check expiry and appearance.Independent verification prevents wrong drug/dose/patient errors.☐
3Prepare and draw the drug under aseptic technique; expel air; cap the needle.Asepsis and air expulsion maintain sterility and correct dose.☐
4Select and palpate the correct site for the age (vastus lateralis for infants; deltoid >3 yr; inner forearm for ID).Age-appropriate sites avoid nerves and vessels.☐
5Clean the site with antiseptic; allow it to dry.Drying antiseptic reduces stinging and ensures antisepsis.☐
6IM: stretch/Z-track, insert at 90°; inject slowly (do not aspirate — current vaccine guidance [CH09]); remove and apply pressure (no rubbing for IM vaccines).Deep 90° insertion reaches the muscle; not rubbing prevents injection-site reactions.☐
7SC: pinch the skin; insert at 45–90°; inject slowly; remove without rubbing.A skin fold avoids the muscle; angle depends on tissue depth.☐
8ID: stretch the skin; insert at 5–15° bevel-up; inject a small bleb/wheal; do not massage or wipe (Mantoux: mark/read on schedule).ID injection stays in the dermis; a wheal confirms technique.☐
9Dispose of the sharps immediately in the puncture-resistant container; never recap two-handed.Immediate disposal prevents needle-stick injury.☐
10Document the drug, dose, route, site, time, and the child's response; observe for local/systemic reaction.Documentation completes the medication cycle and tracks effects.☐

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Volume limits: IM ≤2 mL in a single site (older children); ≤1 mL in infants/smaller children; SC up to 1 mL; ID small (≤0.1 mL).
  • Angles: IM 90°; SC 45–90°; ID 5–15°.
  • Dose formula: Volume (mL) = ordered dose (mg) ÷ concentration (mg/mL) [CH7, CH8].

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Two-nurse dose double-check for high-alert drugs.
  • Do not aspirate for vaccines (current vaccine guidance [CH09]).
  • Dorsogluteal site is avoided in children (sciatic-nerve risk, poor muscle development).
  • Never recap needlessly/two-handed; use the sharps container immediately.

Documentation — What to record

  • Drug, dose (mg and mL), route, site, time, and signature.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student calculates a weight-based dose correctly, selects the correct IM/SC/ID site and angle for the age, and performs the injection on a manikin with correct technique.
