Procedure 20.08 — Oxygen Therapy (Hood, Nasal Cannula, Face Mask)

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Respiratory support · Time: ≈10 min (set-up) · Applies to: hypoxemic children (device by age) · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Provide supplemental oxygen to the hypoxemic child in a safe, humidified, age-appropriate delivery system while monitoring the response.

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • The ordered device, flow/FiO₂, and the target SpO₂ range.
  • Respiratory effort (grunting, retractions, nasal flaring) and skin color — central cyanosis is late.
  • Oxygen source and humidifier water level; the correct device size for the child.

Equipment — What you need

  • Wall/source O₂ with flowmeter and regulator; humidifier (bubbler); hood/cannula/mask of the correct size; extension tubing; pulse oximeter.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Verify the order: device, flow/FiO₂, and target SpO₂ range.Oxygen is a medication — give exactly what is ordered.☐
2Set the flowmeter (L/min) and humidifier; confirm the water level.Humidification prevents mucosal drying, especially at higher flows.☐
3Select the correct-size device; check the fit (hood on the shoulders with a neck seal, cannula prongs in the nares, mask seal).A correct fit delivers the intended FiO₂ without leaks.☐
4Attach the device firmly (secure the line away from the neck); verify free-flow at the patient end.Prevents tubing kinks and accidental disconnection.☐
5Place the pulse-oximeter probe; target the ordered SpO₂ range; check HR and RR.Oximetry confirms the effect of oxygen therapy.☐
6Observe for comfort and signs of hypoxia (retractions, grunting, flaring, color change); treat cyanosis as an emergency.Cyanosis is a late sign — monitor clinical effort, not just the reading.☐
7Prevent skin breakdown (hood edges, cannula cheeks); humidify as ordered.Pressure and dryness cause skin and mucosal injury.☐
8Chart device, flow/FiO₂, SpO₂, and changes; wean only per order.Accurate records guide weaning and avoid hyperoxia.☐
Oxygen hood in an infant

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Nasal cannula: 0.25–2 L/min (humidified in infants).
  • Humidification for flows >2 L/min or prolonged use.
  • SpO₂ target per unit protocol; avoid hyperoxia/hyperoxemia in neonates (retinopathy/BPD risk).

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Oxygen is a medication — verify the ordered concentration/flow each time.
  • No smoking/open flame near oxygen; check the source and tubing.
  • Central cyanosis is a late sign — treat it as an emergency.
  • In RDS, oxygenation is governed by mean airway pressure, not just FiO₂.

Documentation — What to record

  • Device, flow, FiO₂, SpO₂, and clinical response each shift.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student sets the flow, chooses the correct device size, applies pulse oximetry, and states the hazards of hyperoxia in neonates.
