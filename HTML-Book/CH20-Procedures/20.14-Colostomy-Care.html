Procedure 20.14 — Colostomy Care (Pouch Change, Skin, Stoma Assessment)

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Stoma care · Time: ≈10 min · Applies to: child with a colostomy (e.g., Hirschsprung) · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Maintain a healthy peristomal area, support bowel-function recovery, and prevent complications after colostomy for Hirschsprung disease (and other causes) — see Chapter 18.

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • Stoma color and condition (healthy stoma: red, moist, mucosal).
  • Peristomal skin: redness, excoriation, moisture, or bleeding.
  • Output amount, color, and consistency; the pouch fill level.

Equipment — What you need

  • Clean pouch/pediatric pouch (correct size), skin barrier wipes/paste, warm water, gauze, gloves, measuring guide, disposal bag.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Explain to the child/family; prepare gloves and supplies; note the stoma color and condition.Preparation and observation guide a safe, quick change.☐
2Empty the existing pouch through the bottom and rinse; gently remove the pouch from the skin outwards.Emptying first avoids spillage; gentle removal protects the skin.☐
3Clean the peristomal skin with warm water and gauze; shape/trim the skin barrier to the stoma opening.Warm water (not harsh soap) and a correctly trimmed opening protect the skin.☐
4Dry the skin completely.Moisture causes maceration and skin breakdown.☐
5Apply the barrier/paste around the stoma and press the pouch onto it, ensuring a leak-proof seal.A secure seal prevents leakage and skin irritation.☐
6Observe for redness, excoriation, bleeding, or change in stoma color/character; report abnormalities.Early complication detection prevents serious stoma/skin problems.☐
7Document stool output (amount, color, frequency), skin condition, and the pouch-change interval.Output records guide hydration and nutrition.☐
8Teach the caregiver the change procedure, skin-care rules, and when to return (bleeding, dark stoma, no stool/obstruction).Caregiver competence sustains safe home care.☐
Applying a pediatric colostomy pouch over a skin barrier with trimmed opening

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Healthy stoma: red, moist, mucosal; pale, dusky, black, or bleeding needs immediate attention.
  • Change the pouch when 1/3 to 1/2 full or leaking; empty through the bottom for each stool.
  • Stool may be semi-formed or formed depending on the level of the stoma.

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Pale, dusky, black, or bleeding stoma → immediate attention (ischemia/necrosis).
  • Clean with warm water only — no harsh soaps, detergents, or alcohol wipes on the peristomal skin.
  • Involve the stoma care nurse; avoid tight clothing or belts over the pouch.

Documentation — What to record

  • Stoma appearance, output, skin condition, and pouch changes.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student performs a pouch change on a manikin, trims the barrier correctly, and reports stoma complications.
