Procedure 20.12 — Blood Transfusion (Fresh Whole Blood / Packed Red Cells)

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Product administration · Time: ≤4 h per unit (starts slowly) · Applies to: children needing blood products · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Safely administer blood products (packed red cells / fresh whole blood) to restore oxygen-carrying capacity in anemia (thalassemia, sickle cell disease, blood loss) while preventing and promptly recognizing transfusion reactions.

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • Physician's order, cross-match compatibility, and product appropriateness.
  • Two patient identifiers and the blood-bank wristband/cross-match label [CH13].
  • Baseline vital signs (T, HR, RR, BP) and current IV access.

Equipment — What you need

  • Blood product (chilled, undamaged, correct expiry), IV access (microdrip/pump), blood transfusion tubing with filter, normal saline, vital-sign monitor, observations chart.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Verify the physician's order, cross-match compatibility, and product appropriateness.Transfusion begins with an order and a compatible product.☐
2Two-identifier bedside check by two staff: product, unit number, recipient, expiry; inspect the bag (no clots/discoloration/damage).Checking by two licensed staff prevents giving the wrong product.☐
3Take baseline vital signs (T, HR, RR, BP) and record.Baseline values are the reference for detecting reactions.☐
4Prime the transfusion set with the product via filter; connect only to a normal-saline side port.The filter removes clots/wBCs; normal saline is the only compatible flush.☐
5Run slowly initially; stay with the child for the first 15–20 min.Most transfusion reactions occur early; close observation is critical.☐
6Monitor vitals at the ordered intervals and watch for fever, chills, rash, dyspnea, back pain, or hypotension.Early detection of hemolytic/allergic/febrile reactions.☐
7If a reaction is suspected: stop immediately, keep the line open with normal saline, notify the physician, return the bag/unit for investigation, document.Stopping first, then keeping the vein with saline, limits the reaction and preserves access.☐
8Complete the unit within the ordered duration (usually ≤4 h); record the volume transfused and any reactions.Long transfusion times increase the risk of bacterial growth.☐
9Document: unit number, start/stop time, volume, vital signs, and observations.Full documentation is a legal and clinical requirement.☐

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Start slowly: often ≈5 mL/kg/h (or 1–2 mL/min) per protocol.
  • Close observation during the first 15–20 minutes.
  • Complete each unit within ≤4 hours.

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Check the product by two licensed staff; never store blood at the bedside.
  • Do not mix medications or IV fluids (except normal saline) with the unit.
  • Suspected reaction: stop → keep IV patent with normal saline → notify physician → recheck/return the unit [CH13].

Documentation — What to record

  • Product details, start/stop times, volumes, vital signs, and any reaction.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student performs the two-identifier check, starts the transfusion slowly, and correctly reacts (stop/IV saline/notify) to a simulated reaction.
