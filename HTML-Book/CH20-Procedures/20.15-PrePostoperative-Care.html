Procedure 20.15

Procedure 20.15 — Preoperative and Postoperative Care of the Child

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Perioperative care · Time: pre-op ≈15 min; post-op continuous monitoring · Applies to: child scheduled for surgery (see Chapters 18, 19) · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Prepare the child and family for surgery safely (fasting, consent, psychological preparation).
  • Provide systematic postoperative monitoring, pain control, wound care, and early feeding per the surgical condition (see Chapters 18 and 19).

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • Pre-op: NPO start time, consent/explanations, allergies, surgical site, identification band, baseline vitals and weight.
  • Post-op: vital signs, SpO₂, level of consciousness, pain (FLACC/Wong-Baker/0–10 scale by age), wound/dressing, drains/tubes, and input-output.

Equipment — What you need

  • Vital-sign monitor/pulse oximetry, pain scale, IV pump, sterile dressing tray, NG tube + suction (if ordered), emesis basin, appropriate documentation sheet.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Pre-operative: verify NPO start, consent/explanations, allergies; confirm the surgical site and identification band.NPO and site verification prevent aspiration and wrong-site surgery.☐
2Use age-appropriate communication/play to reduce anxiety; document baseline vitals and weight.Psychological preparation and a baseline support safe care.☐
3Confirm the pre-op medication/antibiotic orders and admission data.Correct pre-op orders prevent omission and drug errors.☐
4Post-operative: receive report and monitor vitals, SpO₂, and level of consciousness at the ordered frequency.Systematic monitoring detects early complications (e.g., airway, bleeding).☐
5Assess pain with the age-appropriate scale; give the ordered analgesic and re-assess 30–60 min after.Re-assessment confirms the analgesic worked and guides the next dose.☐
6Monitor the wound/dressing (exudate, bleeding, swelling) and drains/tubes (volume, patency).Early detection of bleeding, infection, or blocked drains.☐
7Begin fluids/feeds per the operation-specific plan (NG decompression → gradual feeds; staged feeding for pyloromyotomy).Feeding plans are procedure-specific (CH19) and prevent aspiration/ileus.☐
8Prevent complications: reposition 2-hourly, observe for fever/ileus/vomiting; document input-output.Repositioning prevents pressure injury; close observation detects ileus and infection.☐
9Document every assessment and the plan; involve the family in discharge teaching (wound care, analgesia, return-if signs).Teaching ensures safe recovery at home.☐

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Preoperative fasting (NPO): clear liquids ≈2 h; breast milk ≈4 h; formula/light meal ≈6 h; solids ≈8 h (per guidelines/unit protocol).
  • Pain re-assessment 30–60 min after analgesia.
  • Repositioning every 2 hours postoperatively.

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Confirm the NPO start time, surgical site, and identification band before surgery.
  • Monitor for opioid respiratory depression when opioids are used for severe pain (CH8).
  • Wound checks: redness, swelling, discharge, or dehiscence must be reported immediately.

Documentation — What to record

  • NPO start, vitals, pain scores, wound status, intake-output, and feeding progression.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student completes a pre-op checklist and performs systematic post-op monitoring with correct pain-scale selection and documentation of staged feeding.
