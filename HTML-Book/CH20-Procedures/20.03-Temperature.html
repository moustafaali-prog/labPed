Procedure 20.03 — Temperature Measurement (Axillary / Tympanic)

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Vital-sign skill · Time: ≈2 min · Applies to: all ages (route by age) · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Obtain an accurate estimate of core temperature safely in children of different ages.
  • Avoid the rectal route (discomfort and risk of injury) in newborns and in immunocompromised or bleeding-risk children.

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • Age (axillary preferred for newborns; tympanic only >3 months).
  • Ear findings: wax, drainage, or recent ear surgery — do not use the tympanic route.
  • History of fever, previous temperature readings, and the child's activity/behavior.

Equipment — What you need

  • Digital thermometer with clean probe covers; tympanic probe tips; lubricant not required for the axillary route.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Explain to the child/family; place a clean probe cover.Consent and infection control; a fresh cover prevents cross-infection.☐
2Axillary route: place the probe tip high in the dry axilla; hold the arm adducted across the chest until the signal.High contact with the skin folds over the probe gives the most accurate axillary reading.☐
3Tympanic route (>3 months): gently pull the pinna back toward the occiput; insert the probe snugly without occluding; press the button.Straightening the canal aims the beam at the tympanic membrane.☐
4Read and record the temperature with the route and time.The route is essential for correct interpretation (axillary reads lower than core).☐
5Compare with the age-based normal range; report values below 36.5 °C or above 38 °C (newborn) / per unit protocol.Out-of-range values trigger medical evaluation in the newborn.☐
6Clean the thermometer; perform hand hygiene.Prevents transmission between patients.☐
Axillary temperature in an infant

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Axillary normal range: 36.5–37.5 °C.
  • Axillary readings typically run 0.5–1 °C lower than core.
  • Fever (rectal equivalent): >38 °C; newborn <36.5 °C or >38 °C needs medical evaluation.

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Do not use the rectal route in newborns, immunocompromised children, diarrhea, or bleeding disorders.
  • Do not use the tympanic route in an ear with wax, drainage, infection, or recent surgery.
  • Document the route used — values cannot be compared across routes.

Documentation — What to record

  • Temperature, route, and time on the vital-sign flow sheet.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student obtains an axillary temperature correctly (probe placement and duration) and identifies the fever thresholds.
