Procedure 20.07 — NG Tube Insertion and Gavage Feeding

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Feeding skill · Time: ≈15–20 min (insertion ≈5 min, feed ≈20–30 min) · Applies to: infants/children unable to feed orally · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Provide nutrition to the infant/child who cannot coordinate suck–swallow–breathe, has poor endurance, or is NPO with an intact gut (e.g., RDS with tachypnea, growth failure, pyloric stenosis pre-op decompression).

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • Feeding order (volume, method, frequency) and the current NPO/fasting plan.
  • Respiratory status — gavage is avoided during marked tachypnea/apnea (aspiration risk).
  • Weight and the planned feed volume; gastric residual if the tube is in place.

Equipment — What you need

  • Tube (5–8 Fr), syringe (for bolus), water/lubricant, stethoscope (confirmatory), pH strips, tape, warm formula/breast milk, pacifier.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Verify the feeding order (volume, method, frequency).Correct volume and method prevent overfeeding and aspiration.☐
2Measure nose-to-ear-to-xiphoid (NEX); mark the tube.NEX approximates the distance to the stomach for the infant.☐
3Position the infant supine with the head slightly elevated (or side-lying); insert gently via nose or mouth.Head elevation reduces reflux and aspiration risk.☐
4Verify placement: aspirate gastric contents; check pH (gastric usually <5.5); confirm if unsure.pH testing is the reliable bedside check — do not rely on auscultation alone.☐
5Secure the tube to the cheek with tape; re-verify position before every feeding.The tube can migrate; re-checking prevents feeding into the airway.☐
6Hold the infant (if stable) during feeding; give warm milk/formula by slow gravity drip over ≈20–30 min; allow pacifier sucking.Slow gravity feeding and nonnutritive sucking support feeding readiness and tolerance.☐
7Position upright/right-side or burp after feeding; monitor for choking, regurgitation, or apnea.Upright/right-side position aids gastric emptying and airway protection.☐
8Flush with a small amount of water if the tube remains in place; close/cap between feeds.Flushing maintains patency and prevents clogging.☐
9Document volume, tolerance, residuals if checked, and the route.Accurate intake records guide the feeding plan.☐
Gavage feeding — tube measurement (NEX) and gravity feeding

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Feeding tube size: 5–8 Fr for infants.
  • Gastric aspirate pH: usually <5.5 (confirms gastric placement).
  • Bolus gavage delivery: slow gravity drip over ≈20–30 min.

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Avoid gavage/nipple feeding during marked tachypnea or apnea (aspiration risk in RDS).
  • Verify placement before every feed — never rely on auscultation alone.
  • Stop the feed and notify the physician on choking, cyanosis, or persistent regurgitation.

Documentation — What to record

  • Type/volume/viscosity of the feed, placement-verification method, residuals, and tolerance.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student inserts a tube on the manikin, verifies placement, and administers a full gavage feed slowly without aspiration.
