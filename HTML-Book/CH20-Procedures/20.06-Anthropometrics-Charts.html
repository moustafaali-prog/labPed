Procedure 20.06 — Anthropometrics and Growth-Chart Plotting

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Assessment skill · Time: ≈5 min · Applies to: all ages (technique varies by age) · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Measure weight, length/height, head circumference (HC), and mid-upper-arm circumference (MUAC) accurately.
  • Plot and interpret results on WHO growth charts to detect normal growth, growth failure, or malnutrition.

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • Age (determines recumbent length vs standing height; MUAC age range 6–59 months).
  • Equipment calibration and the appropriate WHO chart set (weight-for-age, length-for-age, HC-for-age, weight-for-length).
  • Previous measurements for serial comparison (growth velocity).

Equipment — What you need

  • Calibrated infant/standing scales, measuring board/stadiometer, non-stretchable tape, WHO chart kit.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Zero the scale; ask the parent to remove shoes/outer clothes (infant: diaper only).Light clothing and a zeroed scale give an accurate weight.☐
2Weigh (infant supine on the pan; older child standing still); read to the nearest 10 g/0.1 kg.Precise reading detects small changes (daily weight is a fluid-status indicator).☐
3Measure recumbent length (<24 months) with two people on a firm board — crown to heels, legs straight.Recumbent length is used for infants; proper positioning avoids underestimating.☐
4Measure standing height (≥24 months) without shoes, heels together against the stadiometer.Standing height is used from 24 months; technique affects the value.☐
5Measure HC: wrap the tape snugly over the occiput and just above the eyebrows (largest circumference).HC is the index of brain growth in the first years.☐
6Measure MUAC at the midpoint of the left upper arm (between acromion and olecranon); record to the nearest 0.1 cm.MUAC is a rapid malnutrition screen (6–59 months).☐
7Plot measurements on the WHO charts; record percentile/z-score.Placement against percentiles assigns the growth status.☐
8Compare serial values (velocity) — plot the trend over time; report deviations across percentiles.A child crossing percentiles signals growth failure or excess.☐
Measuring recumbent length and HC on the WHO chart

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • MUAC (6–59 months): ≥12.5 cm = normal; 11.5–12.4 cm = moderate acute malnutrition; <11.5 cm = severe acute malnutrition.
  • Weight-for-age, length/height-for-age, HC-for-age, and weight-for-length plotted on the WHO growth charts.
  • Serial plotting: any drop across percentile lines should be flagged and reported.

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Use a firm, stable board for length — never stretch the child.
  • Keep the infant warm and supported while weighing.
  • Use a fresh disposable liner/towel on the scale and board between children.

Documentation — What to record

  • Measurements, the plot date, and the chart type; flag any deviation from the previous percentile.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student performs all four measurements and plots two serial points correctly.
