Procedure 20.11 — IV Cannulation and Infusion-Rate Control (Pediatric)

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Parenteral therapy · Time: ≈10–15 min · Applies to: all ages (site/gauge by age) · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Establish venous access for fluids, medications, or blood.
  • Deliver infusions at exactly the calculated rate using pediatric microdrip sets or infusion pumps.

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • The order: solution, volume, and rate (mL/h) or the infusion duration.
  • Site selection by age (hand/forearm any age; scalp veins in infants; avoid the dominant hand when possible).
  • Child preparation: topical anesthetic if ordered, distraction, and a helper for holding.

Equipment — What you need

  • Small-gauge over-the-needle catheter (24/26 G infants), tourniquet, antiseptic, sterile dressing, T-connector/extension, microdrip set or pump, tape.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Calculate the rate: convert the ordered mL/h or compute gtt/min using the drop factor.gtt/min = (volume (mL) × drop factor) ÷ time (min); or mL/h = total volume ÷ hours [CH7].☐
2Explain to the child/parent with age-appropriate distraction; apply topical anesthetic if ordered.Atraumatic care reduces pain and fear (CH1, CH3).☐
3Select the site; apply a tourniquet or use a helper's hand/gentle hold; don PPE/gloves.Age-appropriate site and restraint prevent movement injury.☐
4Clean with antiseptic; allow it to dry; insert the catheter at 10–30°; observe flashback.Drying antiseptic reduces stinging; flashback confirms vein entry.☐
5Advance, remove the needle, and confirm flow; secure with a sterile dressing.Confirming free flow prevents infiltration.☐
6Set the infusion pump (or count drops) to exactly the calculated rate; prime the tubing to remove air.Air in tubing risks air embolism if disconnected.☐
7Monitor hourly: rate, site (no swelling/pain/redness), patency, and total volume against the plan.Early detection of infiltration/extravasation and phlebitis.☐
8Document the site, gauge, rate, and any pump alarms resolved.Accurate records guide rate verification and troubleshooting.☐
Peripheral IV on an infant's hand with pump

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Pediatric infusions use microdrip (60 gtt/mL) sets or infusion pumps for precise control.
  • gtt/min = (volume (mL) × drop factor) ÷ time (min); mL/h = total volume ÷ hours.
  • Catheter gauge: 24/26 G for infants; small-gauge for children.

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Fluid is a medication in pediatrics — verify the ordered maintenance (Holliday–Segar) volume [CH7].
  • Check the site hourly for swelling, pain, redness, or coolness (infiltration/extravasation).
  • Secure the line to prevent disconnection (risk of air embolism).

Documentation — What to record

  • Cannula gauge/site, IV rate, and hourly totals.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student performs the gtt/min and mL/h calculation for a given order and sets/checks the pump accordingly.
