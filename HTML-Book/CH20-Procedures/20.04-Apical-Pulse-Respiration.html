Procedure 20.04 — Apical Pulse and Respiratory Count

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Vital-sign skill · Time: ≈2 min (full-minute counts) · Applies to: infants and young children <2 years · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Accurately measure heart and respiratory rates in infants and young children.
  • Use the apex as the preferred pulse site after checking respirations before disturbing the child.

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • Activity state (sleeping / awake-quiet / crying) — affects the readings.
  • Previous vital signs, medications, and signs of distress (retractions, grunting, cyanosis).

Equipment — What you need

  • Stethoscope with the correct bell/diaphragm; watch with a second hand.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Allow the child to settle; approach quietly; position comfortably.A quiet state avoids fear-induced tachycardia and tachypnea.☐
2Count respirations for a full minute while appearing to take other vital signs; note rhythm, depth, retractions, or grunting.Infant breathing is irregular with periodic pauses; counting before handling and for a full minute is accurate.☐
3Place the stethoscope at the apex (left chest, 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line).The apex is the point of maximal impulse and the preferred site in young children.☐
4Count the apical pulse for a full minute; note the rhythm and any extra sounds.A full-minute count detects irregularities missed in short counts.☐
5Compare with the age-based normal range; report bradycardia, tachycardia, or irregular rhythm.Early recognition of arrhythmia is essential in children.☐
6Record HR, RR, and the activity state with the time.The activity state is needed to interpret the values.☐
Apical pulse auscultation site in an infant

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Heart rate: newborn 110–160 bpm (up to 180 while crying); infant 100–160 bpm; older child 70–120 bpm.
  • Respiratory rate: newborn 30–60/min; infant 20–40/min; child 16–25/min.
  • Full age-by-age tables: Appendix A (authoritative table for this guide).

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Report bradycardia, sustained tachycardia, or an irregular rhythm.
  • Report increased work of breathing (retractions, grunting, flaring) with the respiratory count.
  • Graph every count with the child's state — a "fast" pulse may be normal if the child is crying.

Documentation — What to record

  • Both full-minute counts with the child's activity state and time.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student obtains a correct apex count and 60-second respiratory count and correctly interprets age-based norms.
