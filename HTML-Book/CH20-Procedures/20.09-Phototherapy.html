Procedure 20.09 — Phototherapy for Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

NICU treatment skill · Time: continuous (checks every shift) · Applies to: jaundiced neonate with phototherapy order · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Lower unconjugated (indirect) bilirubin by photo-isomerization in exposed skin to forms excreted in bile/urine.
  • Prevent kernicterus (see Chapter 11 — Jaundice).

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • The phototherapy order and the bilirubin threshold being treated.
  • Unit irradiance (µW/cm²/nm) and the correct distance from the light to the infant.
  • Weight, hydration state, and baseline temperature.

Equipment — What you need

  • Phototherapy unit (bili lights / bili blanket), eye shields (patches), diaper, incubator/warmer if needed, bilirubinometer/lab slips.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Verify the phototherapy order and the bilirubin threshold being treated.Treatment is threshold-based; confirm the unit is at the correct irradiance.☐
2Confirm the unit irradiance (µW/cm²/nm) and the correct distance from infant to light.The more skin exposed and the higher the irradiance, the more effective the therapy.☐
3Undress the infant to the diaper; apply eye shields firmly but not tightly over both eyes.Eye shields protect the retina; a snug but not tight fit avoids pressure injury.☐
4Position the infant under the light for maximal skin exposure; remove shields only during feeds/care.Maximal exposure maximizes the rate of bilirubin breakdown.☐
5Maintain a neutral thermal environment (incubator/warmer); check temperature frequently.Phototherapy units generate heat; temperature instability is a common side effect.☐
6Maintain fluid intake (breast milk/formula/IV) to compensate for losses; chart input-output and weight.Insensible water loss increases under the lamp; fluids protect against dehydration.☐
7Monitor bilirubin levels per order (q6–12 h) and observe stools (loose green = expected), skin color, and hydration.Loose green stools reflect increased bilirubin excretion — a good sign.☐
8Protect staff/parents from the light (no direct eye exposure); keep the parents informed.Light can damage adult eyes as well; parental teaching reduces anxiety.☐
9On stopping: recheck bilirubin for rebound as ordered; document the last irradiance and level.Rebound hyperbilirubinemia occurs after therapy stops.☐
Phototherapy unit setup with eye shields

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Irradiance per the unit protocol (commonly measured in µW/cm²/nm).
  • Bilirubin rechecked every 6–12 hours during intensive therapy.
  • Expected: loose, green stools and increased fluid needs.

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Never shine the light directly at the infant's eyes; use eye shields correctly.
  • Report temperature instability, dehydration, or a greenish-brown skin discoloration (bronze baby syndrome, rare).
  • Advise parents to keep both eyes shielded while the light is on.

Documentation — What to record

  • Bilirubin values, irradiance, time under light, hydration, stool frequency, and temperature.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student applies eye shields correctly, positions the light, and states the monitoring schedule and rebound check.
