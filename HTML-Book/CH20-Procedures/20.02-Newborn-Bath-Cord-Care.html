Procedure 20.02 — Newborn Bathing and Umbilical Cord Care

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Newborn care · Time: ≈10 min · Applies to: healthy newborn (first bath after cord check) · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Maintain skin integrity and hygiene of the newborn.
  • Prevent hypothermia and promote safe, physiologic cord separation without introducing infection.

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • Temperature stability: delay the first bath until the newborn has been stable for at least 2–4 hours.
  • Skin condition (rashes, birthmarks, desquamation) and cord status (dry, clean, no redness, odor, or discharge).
  • Risk factors: prematurity, cold stress, or an unstable newborn — postpone the bath.

Equipment — What you need

  • Warm water (tested on the inner wrist), hooded towel, washcloth.
  • Basin, clean diaper and clothes, cotton balls/gauze for the cord area.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Check the crib/radiant-heater temperature; gather supplies; close windows.A warm environment prevents drafts and cold stress.☐
2Verify the water temperature (comfortably warm — test on the inner wrist); fill the basin.Water too hot burns newborn skin; too cold causes hypothermia.☐
3Undress the baby keeping the trunk covered; bathe one part at a time with plain warm water (sponge bath).Uncovering one area at a time preserves body heat.☐
4Wash the face (no soap), then the scalp; dry immediately with the hooded towel.Prompt drying minimizes evaporative heat loss.☐
5Wash the body flexures (neck, axillae, groin); dry thoroughly; avoid immersion until the cord falls off.Flexures collect moisture and organisms; immersion delays cord separation.☐
6Cord care: keep the stump clean and dry; fold the diaper below the stump; do not apply antiseptics or powder routinely.Dry care (WHO) prevents infection and speeds separation.☐
7Observe the stump for odor, redness, or discharge; report abnormalities.These are early signs of cord infection (omphalitis).☐
8Dress the baby and recheck temperature after the bath.Detects hypothermia induced by the bath.☐
9Chart the bath, temperature, skin condition, and cord status.Provides a baseline and documents care.☐

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • First bath delayed until temperature is stable for ≥2–4 hours after birth.
  • Axillary temperature maintained 36.5–37.5 °C before and after the bath.
  • Cord separation usually occurs within ≈7–14 days (within the first 2 weeks).

Safety alerts — Protect the child

  • Never leave the baby unattended in or near water.
  • Do not immerse the newborn before cord separation.
  • Never place the baby in hot water — always test on the inner wrist.
  • Keep the cord area dry; fever, redness, or foul odor around the cord requires medical review.

Documentation — What to record

  • Temperature before and after the bath, skin condition, and cord appearance.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student demonstrates correct water-temperature testing, the sponge-bath order, and dry cord care without immersion before cord separation.
