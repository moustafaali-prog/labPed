Procedure 20.01

Procedure 20.01 — Hand Hygiene and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Clinical Pediatric Nursing · for Ward and Laboratory Training

Core ward procedure · Time: ≈5 min · Applies to: all ages · Chapter 20 — Core Clinical Procedures

Purpose — Why we do this procedure

  • Prevent health-care-associated infection (HAI) and cross-transmission of organisms between children, families, and staff.
  • Apply the single most effective measure in infection control (the 5 moments of hand hygiene).

Assessment — What to check before you start

  • Hands: free of jewelry; nails short, natural, and intact skin.
  • The task to be performed and the precaution category in place (airborne / droplet / contact).
  • Which of the 5 moments apply (before contact, before aseptic task, after body-fluid exposure, after contact, after surroundings).

Equipment — What you need

  • Soap (plain or antiseptic) or alcohol-based hand rub; single-use towels.
  • Gloves (clean exam, sterile as indicated), gown, mask/respirator, face shield/goggles.
  • Rigid, puncture-resistant, labeled sharps container.

Procedure Steps

#ActionRationale / precision pointDone
1Remove jewelry; keep nails short and natural.Rings and long nails harbor organisms and tear gloves.☐
2Remove watches/clothing barriers before patient contact.Reduces the surfaces that carry organisms between patients.☐
3Perform hand hygiene at the 5 moments (before/after touching the child, before clean or aseptic tasks, after body-fluid exposure, after surroundings).These moments mark when hands are most likely to transfer pathogens.☐
4Apply the alcohol rub, or wash with soap and water, covering all hand surfaces for the recommended duration.Alcohol rubs are fast and effective on clean hands; soap and water are needed for visibly soiled hands or spore-forming organisms.☐
5Don gloves when contact with blood/body fluids, mucous membranes, or broken skin is anticipated; change between patients and between tasks.Gloves form a barrier; changing them between tasks prevents cross-contamination.☐
6Don gown, mask/respirator, and eye protection when splashing or contact with secretions is anticipated.PPE matched to the route of transmission protects staff and patients.☐
7Dispose of sharps into a rigid, puncture-resistant, labeled container immediately after use.Immediate disposal prevents needle-stick injuries.☐
8Remove PPE in the correct order, then perform hand hygiene last.The final hand hygiene removes contamination picked up while doffing.☐
9Remove gloves by peeling inside-out without touching bare skin.Inside-out removal keeps contaminated surfaces away from the hands.☐

Normal values — Benchmarks for this skill

  • Alcohol hand rub: ≈20–30 seconds to cover and dry all hand surfaces (WHO).
  • Soap-and-water hand wash: ≈40–60 seconds (WHO technique).
  • Standard Precautions apply to all patients, regardless of diagnosis.

Safety alerts — Protect the child and yourself

  • Use soap and water, not hand rub, when hands are visibly soiled or after exposure to spore-forming organisms (C. difficile).
  • Never recap needles two-handed; dispose of sharps immediately.
  • Always perform hand hygiene after removing gloves — gloves are not a substitute.

Documentation — What to record

  • Skill log: date, observed 5-moments compliance, PPE demonstration.

Verification — How you will be checked

  • Student demonstrates the hand-washing technique (observed duration and coverage) and PPE donning/doffing order without contamination.
