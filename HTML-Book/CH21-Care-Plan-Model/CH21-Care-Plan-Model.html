Part VII — Care-Plan Model, Chapter 21

1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, the student will be able to:

  1. Structure a nursing care plan using the NANDA-I nursing process: assessment → nursing diagnosis → goals → interventions (with rationale) → evaluation.
  2. Build a care plan for any of the ward topics in Parts II–V (jaundice, RDS, thalassemia, ALL, hemophilia, pneumonia, diarrhea, Hirschsprung, HPS).
  3. Write measurable, time-bound goals and select age-appropriate interventions.
  4. Use the standard care-plan template attached to every clinical chapter.

4. Writing SMART Goals

  • Specific — "Pass urine at least 1 mL/kg/h" (not "better hydration").
  • Measurable — use numbers (urine output, weight, temperature, SaO₂, pain scale).
  • Achievable, Relevant to the diagnosis, Time-bound ("within 8 h", "by discharge").

Example (fever in pneumonia): goal — "Body temperature within the age normal range (<38 °C axillary) within 12 h of starting antipyretics and fluids." Interventions: antipyretic per order; sponge bath only if very high and per protocol; observe for shivering; increase oral fluids; recheck temperature q2–4 h.

5. Care-Plan Template (use in ward assignments)

  1. Patient context: age, weight, admitting diagnosis, relevant history.
  2. Problem list (from assessment): data + nursing diagnosis.
  3. Goals (SMART): short-term + long-term.
  4. Interventions with rationale: for each, state what you do and why (cite the chapter source).
  5. Evaluation: outcome measures + revision.

6. Case-Study Practice

Give the care plan for: a 4-month-old with bronchiolitis/pneumonia (fever 39 °C, RR 58/min, SpO₂ 90% on room air, feeding poorly).

Sample answer frame: diagnosis Ineffective airway clearance (secretions/inflammation) → goal: RR 30–40/min, SpO₂ ≥95% on O₂ in 24 h → interventions: O₂ per 20.8, position/pulmonary toilet, chest physiotherapy after feeding, antipyretic, fluids, suction only if needed, monitor retractions, escalate if apnea.

7. Multiple-Choice Questions (Key at the end)

  1. The correct sequence of the nursing process is:

A. diagnosis → assessment → planning → implementation → evaluation · B. assessment → diagnosis → goals → interventions → evaluation · C. planning → diagnosis → assessment → evaluation → intervention · D. evaluation → planning → diagnosis → assessment.

  1. "Pass urine 1 mL/kg/h by 8 h" is an example of a:

A. nursing diagnosis · B. SMART goal · C. intervention · D. rationale.

  1. Which phrase belongs in a NANDA-I nursing diagnosis?

A. "The nurse will give ORS" · B. "The risk is 20%" · C. "Deficient fluid volume related to excessive GI losses" · D. "Administer oxygen 2 L/min".

  1. The rationale for giving ORS in moderate dehydration is:

A. to stop diarrhea · B. to sedate the child · C. to replace water and electrolytes lost · D. to prevent vomiting.

Key: 1-B, 2-B, 3-C, 4-C.

8. Key Points

  • The care plan is the record of your clinical thinking, not a paperwork exercise — it guides and evidences nursing care.
  • Always re-assess: evaluation is the step that closes the loop and revises the plan.
